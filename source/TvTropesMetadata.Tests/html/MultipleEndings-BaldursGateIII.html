Multiple Endings / Baldur's Gate III

2Following
Go To

With the countless choices the player can make, there are essentially thousands of endings, with many being a slight permutation of one another.

Due to the nature of the trope, all spoilers below are unmarked.
open/close all folders 

In General 
  • Hero of the Forgotten Realms: Using the Nether Stones, a mind flayer succeeds in bringing the Netherbrain to heel, and destroys it alongside the mind flayer tadpoles were implanted into its victims. Baldur's Gate is spared from destruction and the illithid army swiftly crumble.
  • Absolute Power Corrupts: Either your character or the Emperor takes control of the Netherbrain. If you want to take control of the Netherbrain, then you must either have the Nether Stones already or take the Nether Stones from the mind flayer who currently has them (i.e. Prince Orpheus, the Emperor, or Karlach). What happens after the Netherbrain is hijacked depends on which Origin character the player is. Additionally, if you've consumed too many tadpoles, you'll be forced to pass a Constitution ability check to resist the Netherbrain's attempts to enthrall you in a last-ditch attempt to save itself; fail, and the Netherbrain commences its plan to enact the Grand Design. These are considered the "evil endings" as each character gives in to their worst temptations. The character-specific endings are in their respective folders. The generic endings are thus:
    • The player character rounds up all of the citizens of Baldur's Gate and makes them soldiers for their army. They set off to invade other worlds.
    • The enthralled Baldur's Gate citizens think they are going about their daily lives while they are forced to rebuild their ruined city—including a statue of you.
    • The player character goes on a killing spree, using the Netherbrain's psionics to murder anything with a pulse. At the end, the player lays down on top of a pile of corpses, content at their work.
    • The player character forces everyone to attack each other. They walk through the mayhem, happy to gaze upon the bloodshed.
    • You convince the Emperor to take control over the Netherbrain, enacting his design. You have two options: either become his general or die. Choosing the former has you enthralled, with you and your "Dream Guardian" gleefully killing dissenters together.

Character-Specific Endings

The Dark Urge 
The Dark Urge has several endings depending on two factors: whether they accept Bhaal or refuse his influence.
  • Accepted Bhaal:
    • Sins of the Father: It plays out similarly to the Absolute Power Corrupts Ending, but whereas other Origin characters take control of the Netherbrain for their own purposes, the Dark Urge corrupts the Netherbrain and everyone connected to it with their murderous impulses, exactly as Bhaal intended for them to do from the very beginning. You then either command your companions to take a swan dive off the Netherbrain, or you leave them alive so that they can witness the Dark Urge begin an indiscriminate massacre of everyone inside Baldur's Gate. Bhaal then reveals that this carnage is just the beginning of the all-encompassing bloodbath that is to follow, and shows the Dark Urge a vision of them walking alone in a world reduced to blood, guts, and darkness.
    • If you remain Bhaal's thrall, but destroy the Netherbrain, then Bhaal will become enraged at your disobedience and promise retribution. This leaves you with three options: commit suicide, imprison yourself, or remain free and see what happens. If you commit suicide, then the Dark Urge's soul appears in the Fugue Plane with Withers promising protection for resisting Bhaal. If the player refuses to commit suicide, Bhaal's influence will turn the Dark Urge into a feral shell of their former self. If they are imprisoned, then Withers appears in front of their cage to speak to them; if the Dark Urge refuses imprison themselves, they arrive at Withers' victory celebration six months later to kill everyone. The Dark Urge will become feral no matter if they're a humanoid or a mind flayer.
  • Rejected Bhaal:
    • If the Dark Urge rejects Bhaal completely, he will rip out their divine essence. As they were made directly from Bhaal's blood, this kills them instantly. However, Withers is impressed of their defiance and revives them as a normal person without the Urge, claiming they have grown enough as a person to be more than a reflection of Bhaal's desires.
    • If you reject Bhaal (allowing you to keep control over your soul) but decide to control the Netherbrain, the Dark Urge decides to take power for themselves rather than their father, leading to a standard Absolute Power Corrupts ending.

Astarion 
Astarion starts his story as a vampire spawn fresh out of 200+ year enslavement. The resolution of Astarion's personal quest ends with Cazador's death, one way or another, but how he chooses to go about it can greatly affect his Character Development, for better or for worse.
  • If Astarion is given to the Gur in Act I, killed without being recruited, or Cazador finishes his ritual, then Astarion will turn into a zombie. He is effectively Deader than Dead and cannot be revived via scroll.
  • If Astarion hijacks Cazador's ritual and becomes the Vampire Ascendant (which means sacrificing thousands of other vampire spawn in the process), he'll quickly let his newfound power go to his head and becomes only slightly better than his former master. It's worse if he's romanced, as any romantic feelings he may have had for the Player Character quickly turn into possessive ownership, and he will break things off if they become a mind flayer, stating the main reason to be appearances and implying that he doesn't like he cannot control them anymore (while Spawn Astarion is more concerned about whether the Player Character is even themselves anymore after the transformation). This can be downplayed if he's the Player Character, in which case he can abandon his plans to take over Baldur's Gate or even join Karlach in Avernus to protect her—something that a spawn Astarion can't do, albeit because Avatar Spawn Astarion is forced to hide from the sunlight before he gets the opportunity to talk Karlach out of letting herself die.
  • If he doesn't take over the ritual, he will instead kill Cazador in a fit of rage. However, as he chooses to remain a vampire spawn, he loses his ability to walk in daylight after the tadpole is destroyed, robbing him of his greatest joy and hampering his ability to be with his friends and/or loved one. He even tearfully notes "it was nice while it lasted" before diving for cover. This leads to three potential endings for him in the epilogue:
    • By default, he becomes a traveling-hero. This allows him to drink blood freely without receiving much scorn. He develops a new sense of self and is quite proud of his new calling.
    • If the vampire spawn were freed instead of killed, Astarion can go to the Underdark and act as a leader alongside his other "siblings". If the player character is not Astarion, then they must romance Astarion and be the one to suggest it.
    • Not finishing Astarion's quest leaves him a vulnerable spawn. If he hasn't reconciled with the Gur, then he spends most of his time evading Cazador's hunters. His character development will regress as he tries to manipulate you into helping him kill Cazador. However, if he did reconcile with the Gur, then they hunt down Cazador and kill him together. While he's freed, unlike the other endings, he's more secluded and subdued.

  • If Astarion dominates the Netherbrain, he swaths Baldur's Gate in shadow and props himself up as a "Sun God" who saved the city from complete darkness. However, he is actually a Shadow Dictator expanding his vampiric empire.

Gale 
Gale's questline, which is centered around the Crown of Karsus, can end with him either moving on from the mistakes from his hubris or learning absolutely nothing and seeking to use the crown to become a god. In some endings, his ambition can lead him to think he is more powerful than other gods, which rarely ends in his favor.
  • If the Player Character decides to kill the druids and tiefling refugees in the Emerald Grove, then at the celebration party, he may try to leave, ending his questline early.
  • Gale tries to become a god:
    • If Gale tries to usurp Mystra's domain of magic (which will happen if the player is hostile towards Mystra and doesn't dissuade Gale from his ambitions), then she will almost immediately erase him from existence.
    • If he becomes the god of ambition, almost everyone sees him as a caustic fool. Other gods are particularly hostile towards him. This is mostly due to his pompous demeanor and his use of the Netherese Weave, which caused horrific chaos both recently and centuries ago. Gale will offer his romanced companion the chance to be a god with him and if said companion is Astarion or Karlach, then their life-threatening conditions are removed.
    • If Gale attempts to reforge the Crown of Karsus without the Annals of Karsus, then he dies from the attempt. His Virtual Ghost later appears at Withers' party, lamenting his folly and saying his farewells.
  • Regardless if Gale fishes the crown or leaves it in the Chionthar, he becomes a professor at Blackstaff Academy (if human) and offers the player a chance to lecture his pupils about their exploits. If the player romanced Gale, then he decides to call himself "Gale Dekarios" instead of "Gale of Waterdeep".
    • Should Gale give the crown to Mystra, then she will remove the Netherese orb (if human) or offer to restore his apostolic soul (if mind flayer). If the Netherese orb is removed, he returns to society; if his soul is returned, then he is escorted to Mystra's domain as a member of the recently dead.
    • Alternatively, Gale can simply leave the crown in the Chionthar, which isn't what Mystra specifically requested him to do, but the act still represents him giving up on his plans for godhood and hewing his own path. This outcome also ends in his favor, as while the orb isn't removed from his chest, Mystra nevertheless assures Gale that neither she nor the orb will ever be a danger to him again.

  • Gale can also sacrifice his life by blowing himself up to take out the Netherbrain, which leads everyone, even the githyanki, to declare him a hero. However, during the victory celebration afterwards (if the player, themself, isn't Gale), a Virtual Ghost of Gale left behind for this eventuality explains that there is no way to resurrect him from that blast.
  • If Gale decides to dominate the Netherbrain, he decries the gods while ascending to godhood himself. He intends to use the Cult of the Absolute as his army to subjugate or destroy his rival deities.

Karlach 
No matter what you do, Karlach's questline will end in one of three ways when her infernal engine finally sputters out, and none of them are a happy ending, or at least not a golden one.
  • If the Player Character decides to kill the druids and tiefling refugees in the Emerald Grove, then she will attack the Player Character and their party on-sight, permanently ending her questline in the playthrough.
  • By default, she'll choose to go out peacefully in Faerûn, happy she died out of Zariel's reach. As she never worshiped a deity, she remains unclaimed in the Fugue Plane six months later, with Withers remarking that all of the gods can't bear to look at her bright, shining soul.
  • Karlach can be convinced to go back to Avernus to keep the infernal engine going. Most characters can choose to go with her, or Wyll will go with her so as to protect her from Zariel's henchmen, or they can both accompany her together. However, if the player character is a spawn Astarion, then he cannot join her. This ending features her having a renewed sense of courage and determination at finding a cure for her condition. At the six month reunion, she says that she recently felled a high-ranking cambion with notes on infernal engines, which leads her to believe her infernal heart could be removed in the future.
  • Alternatively, it's possible for Karlach to become a mind flayer, with ceremorphosis destroying the engine. After defeating the Netherbrain, she remains in Baldur's Gate and has a deal with the local healer so she can feed on hospice patients. While she's happy to be alive in Faerûn, her personality is noticeably different by the six month mark, with her even remarking that her original identity dilutes a little bit with every brain she consumes. There's also the question if she's even Karlach anymore or just the parasite that thinks it's Karlach.

  • If Karlach dominates the Netherbrain, she opens up portals to Avernus and unleashes a horde of devils upon Baldur's Gate as retribution for their blind faith of Gortash and turning her into an outcast. The power of the Netherbrain allows her to rip out her infernal engine and replace the hole in her chest with a fresh heart, getting rid of the ticking clock that plagued her.

Lae'zel 
Lae'zel's plotline revolves around Queen Vlaakith and her lies, as well as the truths about githyanki society. Lae'zel experiences the githyanki's xenophobic behavior for aiding istiks and containing an illithid parasite in her head. She sees first-hand how corrupt most githyanki are, as they do not seem to value duty, loyalty, or cooperation as much as recognition or pride (something she, herself, is guilty of), with the only real uniting factor being their dogmatic eradication of the Grand Design and their hatred of the githzerai. Whether she recognizes this or chooses to live in denial changes which ending occurs.
  • There are several points in the game where Lae'zel can become permanently hostile, leading to her death either on-screen or off-screen. If you attack Ch'r'ai W'wargaz or his subordinates before they attack you, or leave before entering the Astral Prism, she will turn against you if her approval is too low. If Lae'zel is loyal to Orpheus, she will also become hostile if you or the Emperor attempt to eat him without convincing her it's a good idea first.
  • By default, Lae'zel will remain loyal to Vlaakith if the Creche questline isn't completed. However, even after the truth about Vlaakith's reign is revealed, the player can still go out of their way to screw Lae'zel over and have her remain fanatically loyal to Vlaakith. This will cause her to absolutely despise Orpheus and see him as a traitor. In the event you choose to side with Orpheus instead of the Emperor, Lae'zel will try to kill Orpheus at the docks. If you cannot convince her to still her sword, the only way to prevent her from killing him is by killing her first. If Lae'zel makes it to the end of the game, Vlaakith absorbs Lae'zel's soul during her "ascension", erasing her from existence.
  • If you convince Lae'zel to turn back on her faith, her view of the githyanki and its leadership are irreparably shattered. This makes her realize her kind is not perfect or as proud as she thought they were. In addition to this, her standing with the githyanki changes depending on whether you side with Prince Orpheus or the Emperor toward the end of the game.
    • If you side with Prince Orpheus and Lae'zel goes to the Astral Plane, Lae'zel tries to repair the relationship with the githzerai and the githyanki. She does this whether Voss or Prince Orpheus are alive or if Orpheus becomes a mind flayer, as the githzerai will reach out to her and the rebellion. You can also join the rebellion regardless of your race—even if you have turned into a mind flayer. For the sixth month party, both she and her partner appear as an astral projection, and she informs everyone that she has a meeting with Zaerith Menyar-Ag-Gith, leader of the githzerai, and that prospects of the rebellion are looking good.
    • If you side with Prince Orpheus and Lae'zel decides to stay in Faerûn, she lives a relatively normal life while also going on parties to eliminate Vlaakith's strongholds.
    • If you side with the Emperor, Prince Orpheus' supporters decry her for regicide and are out for her blood, with the implication being she'll be hunted by them for the rest of her life.

  • Lae'zel dominates the Netherbrain and its thralls to give herself a dragon and wage war against Vlaakith. This war is implied to be rather short as the very next scene features a terrified Vlaakith surrounded by gith soldiers. She plans on becoming the new ruler of the githyanki empire.

Shadowheart 
Regardless of how you pursue Shadowheart's questline, she will not end up in a good place, particularly where the end of her personal quest is concerned.
  • If she commits to the Sharran priesthood and becomes a Dark Justiciar, her final test sees her standing in front of her parents. Shar restores her memories of who they are and tells her that her final test is to kill them. Shadowheart either acquiesces, which breaks her so completely that she begs Shar to erase her memories again, or turns against Shar at the last minute to save her parents, causing her to suffer Shar's curse for the rest of her life and lose the support of the Sharran cult she'd just taken over. Shadowheart's loyalty to Shar will also affect your romance with her by the endgame, as she becomes more distant thanks to dealing with Shar's demands.
  • Alternatively, you can convince Shadowheart to turn away from Shar, converting to her counterpart and rival Selûne in the process and becoming an enemy of the Sharran priesthood. Like in the evil path, Shadowheart finds her parents, but both Shar and they confirm that the only way to be free of Shar is to kill them. Either she does and is freed at the cost of her parents' lives, or she saves them but remains bound to Shar's curse, though she considers this a minor inconvenience compared to getting her family back.
  • Like with Wyll, it's also possible for Shadowheart's story to end horribly in the Selûnite path if you cut a deal with Viconia and sell her out to the cult, which will cause her to lose all her memories, even those of your party and everything you've been through together, and become completely subservient to Viconia and Shar.
  • If Shadowheart dominates the Netherbrain, infuriated by what the war between Selûne and Shar cost everyone, she is set on eliminating both gods' followers in an attempt to kill them. The ending implies she will ascend to greater heights before condemning her former Sharran cloister to death.

Wyll 
  • If the Player Character decides to kill the druids and tiefling refugees in the Emerald Grove, then he will attack the Player Character and their party on-sight, permanently ending his questline in the playthrough.
  • It's possible for Wyll's story to end horribly if you kill Mizora during Act 2, in which case Wyll will be dragged off to Avernus with her and suffer a Fate Worse than Death by becoming a Lemure.
  • Even if Mizora doesn't die and the player helps Wyll in convincing her to break the contract, Mizora will find a way to weasel out of it by providing Wyll with a Sadistic Choice in Act 3: either he renews his pact into an eternal one and saves his father, or he breaks it but condemns his father to a near-certain death.
    • If the contract isn't broken, Wyll ensures his father's safety, but in return, he becomes Mizora's servant in full and "assists" her in fighting the Blood War.
    • Wyll can Take a Third Option by breaking the pact once and for all but still managing to rescue his father, in spite of Mizora's attempts to stymie his efforts.
  • His actions regarding both his patron and his father can also influence how he'll move forward with his life after the Absolute is dealt with. He can continue his adventures as the Blade of Frontiers, or turn away from monster-hunting and take up his father's mantle as the Grand Duke of Baldur's Gate, or rechristen himself as the Blade of Avernus. And provided that he ended his contract with Mizora, it's only the very latter scenario that gives him the option of getting some long-awaited revenge on her.
  • If Wyll takes control of the Netherbrain, he usurps Baldur's Gate's leaders to usher a new era of peace by any means necessary. He turns his father and several people in high positions into mind flayers with the implication he'll use them in his new army/enforcers of his rule.

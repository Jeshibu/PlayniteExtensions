White Elephant Unit
5 Following

Go To
  • Main
  • Laconic
  • Create Subpage

Your opponent can't refuse your generous donation.
—Magic: The Gathering, ruling for the card Harmless Offering

According to legend, the King of Siam had an ingenious way to passive-aggressively dispose of potential rivals. You see, he maintained a herd of sacred white elephants. They could not be sent to work and were very expensive to feed, but the king could afford it. A lesser noble or courtier, on the other hand, would be hard-pressed to feed even one of them. So if someone was causing trouble in court, the king would entrust him with the care of a sacred white elephant and let it eat him out of house and home.

Similarly, some games have items, cards, or units that the owner might prefer to offload on their opponent so they have to deal with it. There are two common ways a White Elephant Unit ends up in an opponent's possession. A "pure" white elephant is given to an opponent as part of its own effect, while a "combo piece" white elephant has to be given away manually through some other effect.

A pure white elephant unit is usually flavoured as something undesirable, such as a cowardly fighter or a curse you suffer. They often have explicit detrimental effects like making you lose points or resources, and if you have a limited number of slots available, they'll clog up one of them. However, there are also some subtler examples: for instance, giving your opponent a weak Vanilla Unit can be terrible for them in a game where losing any three units is an instant loss. Some white elephants are also Not Completely Useless for the target or have mild positive effects in addition to the detrimental ones. You have to be more careful about giving out these to avoid inadvertently helping the target.

Combo piece white elephant units are more varied. They can be purely detrimental Joke Items or something that's rendered Awesome, but Impractical thanks to a hideous downside. However, combining these with otherwise-baffling "give your opponent something for free" effects makes both Not Completely Useless. With that said, these combos often end up being Awesome, but Impractical, as you need to run both the white elephant and an effect that gives it to your opponent, so why not just run two units that actually help you instead? A better option may be to exploit Equivalent Exchange-type items that let you force a white elephant on them and steal something useful from them.

For obvious reasons, white elephant units tend not to show up in games where players can get rid of their own units at will. Some of these games still have them and slap on a clause like "you can't remove this unit", however.

Can involve Manipulating the Opponent's Deck, but not always. Compare Poison Mushroom (power-ups with bad effects), Cursed Item, Joke Item, and Joke Character. See also Deck Clogger for cards that clog up your deck in Deckbuilding Games — if they're forced on you by opponents, they're also white elephants. Contrast Unwanted Gift Plot for narratives about people receiving unwanted gifts, and The Millstone for non-game examples of characters

Examples:

    open/close all folders 

    Anime & Manga 
  • Yu-Gi-Oh!: During Marik and Jonouchi's duel, Marik summons his Lava Golem to Jonouchi's side of the field. At the end phase of each turn, Lava Golem inflicts 700 damage to Jonouchi's life points as long as it remains on his side of the field. However, Jonouchi is able to turn this around by offering Lava Golem and two other monsters he controls to summon Gilford the Lightning.
  • Yu-Gi-Oh! GX: When Jaden has a rematch with Sartorius during season 4, Sartorius secretly inserts the card Arcana Force 0 - The Fool into Jaden's deck before the duel and then uses another card to force him to summon it, which allows Sartorius to choose the random effects of his own cards and repeatedly attack The Fool to deal damage as though he was attacking Jaden directly.

    Party Games 
  • The party game White Elephant, often played at Christmas gatherings, is a reference to the legend. Players bring wrapped gifts (with clever players deliberately packaging their presents to disguise their true contents) and draw numbers, then take turns picking out presents in numerical order. On their turn one can either choose a wrapped gift and unwrap it, or take another player's unwrapped present if it's something they'd like; there is usually a rule in place that states that an item can only be "stolen" from someone else twice before it is permanently taken out of play and kept by the last person to request it. At least one of the presents is the titular White Elephant—a useless piece of junk that nobody actually wants—and trying to avoid getting it is part of the fun, as many families make a tradition of using the game as an excuse to unload unwanted junk on their relatives.

    Tabletop Games 
Board Games
  • Abyss: The Kraken Expansion Pack introduces Nebulis, the black pearls. At the end of the game, they're worth -1 point each, and whoever has the most gets an additional penalty of -5. Nebulis can be used as currency, but they're awkward to get rid of because you can only spend a single Nebulis in a transaction, and even then you can only do it if you're out of normal pearls. The most reliable way to get rid of them is by purchasing an Ally off of an opponent, which costs 1 pearl (for the first Ally that turn anyway), and saddles them with a Nebulis. Of course, they can do the same to you later on...
  • Chess: According to a legend, for a time, there was no rule stating that if a pawn reached the back spaces of the board, they must promote to the same color. Thus, there was always the slim possibility that a savvy player would promote a pawn to the opposing player's color to deprive them of spaces to move to. For example, this chess puzzle.
  • Everdell:
    • The Fool is a Critter who's played into an opponent's city. It's worth negative points, and worse, it clogs up a valuable city slot. There are ways to get rid of it, but that means foregoing the chance to get rid of something else.
    • The Rugwort Pack has Rugwort the Rowdy, who's played into an opponent's city. Not only is it worth 0 points and clogs up a city spot, but Rugwort's user also gets to steal one of the target's production cards when they play it and to add insult to injury, Rugwort itself is a production card with a mandatory effect that forces its owner to discard cards.
  • Lords Of Waterdeep: Mandatory quests are found within the intrigue deck and are intended to be given to another player. They prevent the receiving player from completing regular quests while in play and generally have a very poor ratio of points scored compared to resources expended.
  • Res Arcana: Downplayed. The fact that the Fiery Whip (Elan), Jeweled Statuette, and Hand of Glory (Death) also give essences to your opponents is normally a downside. However, there's a niche situation where it can be exploited: if they have no easy way to spend it, and you have one of the artifacts whose effect scales based on how much Elan or Death an opponent has (Hypnotic Basin for Elan, Treant for Death).
  • Seasons: When you summon Naria the Prophetess, you draw as many cards as there are players, keep one and deal one to each opponent. This often gives them undesirable cards, which is a problem because you're penalized for each unused card in your hand at the end of the game.
  • Wingspan (2019): Downplayed. The Blue Rock-Thrush and the Spotted Dove are worth 0 points and have the ability that when they're activated, if they have no birds to the right of them, you can draw 3 cards and play them to the mat belonging to the player on your left/right, with you choosing where. This is mostly a "friendly" player interaction bird that gives the opponent the choice to activate it to draw cards or keep it around to fill up a habitat. However, in the late game, the fact that it clogs up a spot on your board can be a problem. If it's the last turn and you're planning to play the valuable Atlantic Puffin (a Wetlands-only bird) into your last Wetlands slot, you really don't want a Blue Rock-Thrush/Spotted Dove to block it.

Card Games

  • Abandon All Artichokes: You want to remove the ten Artichoke cards that have no effect and make up your starting deck, and win if you draw a hand consisting entirely of non-artichokes. There are two ways to give artichokes to opponents:
    • The Beet makes you and an opponent of your choice reveal a random card from your hand. If both are artichokes, they're composted, otherwise the players swap them. This means that you can give an opponent an artichoke and get a useful (or at least harmless) card in return.
    • The Eggplant has you compost an artichoke and the Eggplant itself. Then everyone passes two cards to the left, which means a chance to pass someone else up to two artichokes.
  • Dominion (2008) has quite a few cards that give opponents undesirable cards that just end up clogging up their deck.
    • The most obvious white elephant card is Curse, which is given out by a lot of attack cards and gives -1 Victory Point per Curse.
    • The Dark Ages Expansion Pack introduces Ruins, which aim to be more interesting penalty cards by consisting of five possible Action cards with very weak effects. Two attack cards give each other players a Ruins, and one card gives its user Ruins as a drawback.
    • The starting Coppers, while vital for your first few buys, quickly become outclassed and undesirable. A few attack cards specifically give opponents Coppers, and several cards can potentially saddle others with Coppers.
    • The three starting Estates are almost never desirable, as they have no immediate effect on the game when drawn, and their mere 1 point does not make up for how many draws they shut down. No attack specifically gives out Estates, but there are some ways to force them on opponents.
  • Doomlings has certain cards such as Venomous that you can play, with a negative point value. If you play this, you have to give this card to another player, causing that negative hit point value to affect them.
  • Fluxx: Creeper cards block the owner from winning the game while they're in play. They're normally obtained by drawing cards, but some card effects allow a player to inflict a Creeper on their opponent; one small mercy is that they don't count against the per-turn draw and play limits.
  • Hearts is a trick-taking game played with a standard deck of playing cards in which the objective is to collect the least points. Fortunately most cards are worth zero points but each card in the suit of hearts is worth one point, and the Queen of Spades is worth a whopping thirteen. Daring players can also invert the trope by making a bid to collect every point by having the entire heart suit and the Queen of Spades in their hand at once, making the other cards the White Elephant Units; this tactic is known as "shooting the moon" and, if successful, is an Instant-Win Condition.
  • Old Maid is a traditional game played with an odd number of cards from a standard 52-card deck.note You can also use any other deck whose cards form matching pairs, and some dedicated decks exist. Every card, save the titular old maid, is part of a matching pair. Players attempt to offload cards, either by playing a match or passing to another player, until no more matches can be made. Whoever has the old maid at the end of the game loses.

Collectible Card Games

  • BattleTech: The collectible card game spinoff from the 90s had the card Rookie Pilot, which could only be played on an enemy's mech that didn't already have a pilot. The equipped mech got its damage reduced for the rest of the game, and unlike other pilot cards, Rookie Pilot couldn't be transferred between mechs.
  • Magic: The Gathering:
    • Akroan Horse, a reference to the Trojan Horse, is given to an opponent while played. If you get it, you're stuck with an underwhelming creature that churns out tokens for everyone else.
    • Xantcha, Sleeper Agent is given to an opponent while played. While she at least has good stats, she has two bad effects: The first is that she has to attack if able, but can't target you or your stuff. The second is that anyone can pay 3 mana to draw a card while her controller loses 2 life.
    • Downplayed with Aura Curses — they have detrimental effects and can be attached to opponents, but they're technically considered yours.
    • While the normal, black-border rules don't allow you to put undesired cards into your opponent's deck, some supplementary cards feature such mechanics:
      • The Self-Parody set Unhinged introduces Letter Bomb, which shuffles itself into an opponent's deck. It not only shuts down a draw but deals 19.5 damage when someone is unfortunate enough to draw it. Since the starting life total is 20 and Letter Bomb is too expensive to be cast early, this will probably kill them immediately.
      • The playtest card Gunk Slug shuffles three Gunk cards into an opponent's deck. All you can do with one of them is pay 4 to discard it and draw a new card.
    • The card Blim, Comedic Genius can make any card this, as its effect forces opponents to gain control of a permanent of yours, but then they lose life and discard cards equal to the number of permanents they control but don't own.
    • Illusions of Grandeur gives the player 20 extra life, only to take it away when the card leaves play. And the card has an increasingly expensive upkeep. Naturally, people figured out that you could Donate it to someone else, giving them the problem after you've taken the benefit.
    • Delusions of Mediocrity is a nerfed version of Illusions of Grandeur.
    • Abyssal Persecutor has the effect of you not being able to win and your opponents not being able to lose.
    • Deadpool, Trading Card is an interesting take on this by having purely detrimental abilities while on the board, along with the ability to swap his text box with any other creature's when he enters play, so now your opponent's formerly-good creature is stuck damaging them every turn and they can't reasonably get rid of it except by wasting mana and letting everyone else draw a card.
  • Yu-Gi-Oh!:
    • Parasite Paracide can shuffle itself into your opponent's deck, and when they draw it, they're forced to summon it and take 1000 damage.
    • Dustons are an archetype that wants to summon crappy monsters to the opponent's side of the field to clog up their limited number of Monster Zones. These monsters have bad stats, detrimental effects, and protection from being used as summoning fodder.note Note that they predate Link Summoning and do not have protection from being used as Link material. Ojamas have a similar gimmick.
    • Creature Swap has each player choose a monster to give to their opponent. This can be exploited to give them a monster with bad effects like Number 30: Acid Golem of Destruction (which is likely to shut down their strategy and kill them in a few turns).
    • Griggle and Ameba are monsters that have effects that are only triggered if control of them is given to the opponent using Creature Swap or a similar card. Griggle heals you by a huge amount while Ameba does damage to the opponent. Similarly, the equip card Vengeful Servant inflicts damage equal to the ATK of the monster it is equipped to if control of that monster is switched.
    • Mystical Refpanel switches the target of a Spell Card that targets a player. This means that you can use it on bad Spell Cards that target you in order to force the effect on your opponent.
    • The Kaiju archetype, Lava Golem, and The Winged Dragon of Ra - Sphere Mode all have been used for the same general reason, respectively being able to tribute 1, 2, or 3 of the opponent's monsters. Because there are very few effects in the game that prevent tributing, this is a reliable way to hinder or even brick the opponent's strategy.
      • The Kaijus require their in-archetype Field Spell to use any of their special effects. Without it, they are just generic beatsticks with an odd summoning condition. Having one or two weak Kaijus in your deck is a great way to replace the opponent's boss monster with something far less problematic.
      • Lava Golem has an impressive 3,000 ATK, but it takes two of your opponent's monsters to summon and does 1,000 LP of damage to its unwilling owner every Standby Phase. By far the most infamous White Elephant Unit in the game because its only requirement is that your opponent needs more than one monster for their strategy.
      • Sphere Mode takes the idea to its logical conclusion by being unable to do anything, the opponent losing three of their monsters for what is effectively a monster token that can't even attack. If they fail to remove it by the end of their turn, it will return to its owner's side of the field, potentially letting them summon a 4000 ATK Winged Dragon of Ra into a wide open field. (Unless the opponent also has a Winged Dragon of Ra in their deck. Then things get awkward.)
    • Nibiru, the Primal Being is a handtrap that generously fuses all your opponent's monsters into one big monster token for them. Never mind any effects those monsters had or the concept of card advantage.
    • Arcana Force 0 - The Fool has 0 ATK and can't be destroyed by battle or changed to defense mode without a card effect, which allows it to be attacked over and over again to deal damage. Saiou used it as this trope as a window to deal damage to Judai in the 4th season of GX. The anime version of this card was even more powerful, as it allowed the opponent to choose the results of their coin tosses.
    • Cards like Branded Expulsion and Imperial Princess Quinquery have the ability to summon monsters to the opponent's field, allowing you to apply this trope to monsters of your choosing. Notably they have been used on the likes of Gimmick Puppet Nightmare and Artifact Mjollnir to subject the opponent to their crippling locks that were meant to apply to their owner as counterbalancing factors.

    Video Games 
Action RPG
  • Arcanum: NPCs tend to pick up loose items and even equip them regardless of what they are, which can lead to some interesting situations (like when a band of mysterious assassins announces you're going to die, one of them is off picking flowers before resuming his position like nothing happened) and exploitations:
    • Dropping railroad spikes in front of Lukan the Witless and his ogres greatly reduces their damage, making them a much easier Beef Gate.
    • Dropping a cursed or self-poisoning item in front of an NPC (usually a shopkeeper) lets you wait and loot their inventory from their corpse once they've killed themselves, without it counting as you murdering them.
  • Kingdom Hearts Re: Chain of Memories: During Riku's boss fight against Zexion, the latter will steal Riku's cards. When taking them back, Zexion will also drop fake cards with the image of Riku's Keyblade mirrored. If Riku unwittingly attempts to use one of the fake cards, he will be stunned and left open to Zexion's attacks.
  • The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt: In the Gwent minigame, Spies are a specific type of card that enter on the opponent's side of the battlefield, adding to their power in the process, but draw their owner a card. To balance this, most Spy cards have a fairly high power... except for The Mysterious Elf card, which has a power of 0, meaning that it's basically "Play this card, draw a card". It's a Hero card as well, meaning it can't be targeted by Medics (which revive units in the discard pile) or Decoys (which return a card to your hand, allowing it to be replayed).

Card Battle Games

  • Blox Cards: Anthrested Knights. They're token fighters that are usually created by transforming another fighter into one. They have no effect, other than giving all Anthrested fighters 200 Health when they die, which will usually benefit your opponent. On the bright side, they aren't inert, meaning that they can be used for the Colourless gen.
  • Card City Nights 2: Skull Card X is made to be given to the enemy, since, when activated, it damages its owner whenever they play a card, it's not like some other cards that are defined as belonging to the opponent when placed by the one who placed them down.
  • Hearthstone: Heroes of Warcraft:
    • Weasel Tunneler and Bad Luck Albatross shuffle weak 1/1 minions into the opponent's deck, leading to wasted draws.
    • Treachery is a Warlock spell that has you choose one of your minions to give to your opponent. It is often used on a minion with a negative effect, like Howlfiend or Fel Reaver.
    • Some cards are shuffled into the deck and deal damage to the player who draws them. Bomb and Mine can be added to the opponent's deck to make them reluctant to draw more cards, and Corrupted Blood is added to both decks at the same time by Hakkar the Soulflayer.
      • For the TITANS expansion, Death Knight received a package of cards revolving around Plagues, which are shuffled into the opponent's deck like Bombs. They each deal 2 damage to the opponent, plus an additional effect: the Blood Plague heals you for 2 Health, the Unholy Plague summons a 2/2 Undead for you, and the Frost Plague makes their next card cost 1 more. The Legendary, Helya, shuffles all three Plagues into your opponent's deck, and makes Plagues they draw shuffle back into their deck.
    • Cursed! and Abyssal Curse can be added to the opponent's hand by other cards, and deal damage to the player holding them every turn. Cursed! stays in the hand until the player spends mana to discard it. Abyssal Curse will discard itself after two turns, but gets progressively more dangerous over the course of the game, since every one the opponent receives will deal 1 more damage per turn than the previous one.
    • Excavated Evil deals three damage to all minions, then shuffles itself into the opponent's deck. It's intended as a counter to aggro-decks that rely on lots of cheap minions - not only is the initial effect likely to wipe the board, but if drawn again it's effectively a dead draw since playing it would probably wreck the opponent's strategy a 2nd time.
  • Marvel Snap:
    • Green Goblin and Hobgoblin are cards with -3 Power and -8 Power respectively, which have the On Reveal effect of flying over to the opponent's side of the board. Not only does this lower the total Power at their location, it also takes up one of the four spots they have to play cards there. However, this can backfire if the opposing player has a card that can destroy their own cards (Carnage, Deathlock) or worse, something to send back the Goblin (Odin, Viper).
    • Black Widow adds a Widow's Bite to your opponent's hand, which prevents the opponent from drawing cards until they play it. It can be played for free, but the Bite has -1 Power and takes up a spot on the board.
    • White Widow is a mix of both Black Widow and the Goblins, as she adds a -4 Power Widow's Kiss to the opponent's side of the board, which only goes away once the opponent has filled their side of that location. 

Eastern RPG

  • Pokémon:
    • The move Bestow gives its owner's item to its target, and the moves Trick and Switcheroo make its user switch held items with its target. This can be combined with items that have detrimental effects, like the Flame Orb (which burns its user), Toxic Orb (badly poisons its user), Iron Ball (halves the user's speed), and Lagging Tail (makes the user always move last). The most common one is the special case of Choice Scarf, which boosts the user's speed while locking the user into the first move they pick. It's often used for its beneficial effect, but can also be used as a white elephant for an opposing Pokémon (often a defensive one using mostly non-attacking moves) that doesn't care about being fast and needs to use all of its moves.
    • Sticky Barb is a self-damaging item that can go to the opponent without any need for Trick or Switcheroo, as it transfers to the opponent when they hit the holder with an attack that makes contact.
    • The ability Truant causes its bearer to skip every other turn. Durant, one of the wielders of this ability, can use Entrainment to stick it on the opponent and cripple them until they switch out.

Party Game

  • Mario Party: Some of the more hostile characters give you harmful items.
    • In the original game, all of the items you received if you landed on a Bowser Space fall under this trope. Bowser promises that whatever he'll give you is rare and powerful (such as an inner tube in Yoshi's Tropical Island or a golden statue in DK's Jungle Adventure), but they're ultimately trash that just waste your coins.
    • The Evil Woody from Mario Party 3 can give players the Minus Coin or Reverse Block fruit as chosen by the player if he is visited. The latter can be a subverted case under specific conditions, though—if a player has recently passed a Star Space and now has enough coins to purchase one, or has one appear behind them after another player buys a Star in a different location, they can get a second chance at reaching it.
    • In Mario Party 6, the Evil Woody from Mario Party 3 haunts the top of the Towering Treetop board at night. If visited, he takes away coins.
    • In Mario Party 7, Bowser can turn one of the shops on the boards into his own store (known as Bowser City in the manual), with his store having Golden Bowser Statues (which do nothing and are lost immediately upon obtaining) or Koopa Kid Orbs (which create another trap that can be destroyed by the player's own traps, the Egg Orb, or the Star moving there). Both types of items set players back 20 coins.

Roguelike

  • Peglin: At higher Cruciball levels, you may be forced to start with a Terriball (extremely small and weak) or Horriball (large, non-damaging, cannot be disposed of), with the implication being that these are not orbs you want to be using. Pebballs may also qualify if one is sufficiently cynical.
  • For a player-versus-enemy example, Rogue Adventure has several bosses that can force you to shuffle detrimental cards into your deck:
    • Mushroom Twins (Forest miniboss) force you to take Poison Mushroom cards into your deck when you harm them. These deal damage to you when played.
    • The Evil Tree (Forest boss) gives you Evil Berries. These deal damage when not played.
    • Garoum (Undersea miniboss) gives you Red Fish. These heal you and give you MP, but also heal Garoum by 5 when played.
    • The Sun King and Sun Queen (Desert boss) give you Dark Spheres whenever you harm the Queen. These deal damage if not played, but buff the King's attack power if you do.
  • Peglin forces these onto players challenging the progressively more difficult Cruciball:
    • The Terriball is a tiny 1/2 orb with no special effects and no upgrades added to players' deck in Cruciball 2. It's typically one of the first orbs to go whenever a player removes an orb from their deck.
    • The Horriball is an even worse orb given to players at Cruciball 17, as not only does it have -1/-2 stats to counteract any effects or relics you might find, it cannot be removed by any means. Sure, it's big, but that doesn't mean much when it's dealing little to no damage throughout the entire game.
    • To a lesser extent, the Pebballs every player starts with are these. There are events in each of the three acts that force players to choose between adding basic Pebballs to their deck or another undesirable choice, which means they fall into this trope. While certain builds can benefit from more Pebballs, the orbs themselves are typically too weak for later acts, so players will usually remove them from their decks as soon as possible.

Turn-Based Strategy

  • Heroes of Might and Magic: The Fizbin of Misfortune is a Cursed Item and the only item in the game with a negative effect, giving the hero unfortunate enough to be carrying it a -2 to their luck stat. One possible way to use it to weaken your enemies is to give it to a hero with a weak army and send them to lose a battle with an enemy hero so that they're forced to claim it, before sending your main army in.

    Western Animation 
  • Craig of the Creek: In "Bring Out Your Beast", Craig battles Turner who uses a card called Beast Snare that steals an opponent's card when it's played. The only way Craig can counter it is by using a card that damages its players when in their hand. So when Turner steals the card, she gets damage instead.
